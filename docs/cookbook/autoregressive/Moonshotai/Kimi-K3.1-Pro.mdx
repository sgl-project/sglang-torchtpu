Pick your hardware, deployment mode and serving strategy to generate the launch command:

- **Low-Latency (TP8)**: the fastest replies with a few users at once.
- **High-Concurrency (DCP8)** (B300, GB300, Unified): the most requests and context per GPU, with the KV cache split across the eight GPUs.
- **Balanced** (decode role, other platforms): good speed with several users at once, for typical multi-user serving.
- **High-Throughput**: the most tokens per second across many users, for batch jobs and many concurrent agent sessions.
- **Prefill / Decode**: run the two phases on separate nodes (see [PD disaggregation](#pd-disaggregation)). The prefill role offers Low-Latency (TP8) and High-Throughput (PP4 × PCP2); the decode role offers Balanced (DCP8 · EP2) and High-Throughput (DCP8). On MI350X / MI355X, both roles offer High-Throughput (DCP8).

## Agentic Long-Context Deployment

Agentic workloads, such as coding agents and other multi-turn, tool-using assistants, send long prompts whose prefixes grow and repeat from turn to turn. Serving them well depends on reusing the KV cache across turns, offloading it beyond GPU memory, and keeping each session on the workers that already hold its prefix. This section gives complete launch commands for such deployments on GB300 with Dynamo and SGLang, and on MI355X with SGLang, measured on the AgentX agentic-coding trace replay. Pick a deployment shape, concurrency, KV cache offloading mechanism and router to get every process of the deployment as a raw command, one tab per process in launch order.

<Note>
  These commands are a **status snapshot** (2026-11-03) of a GB300 tuning campaign that is still running. They are not an InferenceX submission. Each was measured with the DFlash acceptance length fixed to the draft's expected value (see the note under each configuration). The aggregated deployment at concurrency 1 missed its time-to-first-token target; the other seven points met theirs.
</Note>

- **Router.** Every configuration was measured behind Dynamo (`dynamo.frontend` with `dynamo.sglang` workers and the KV-aware router with session affinity). The SGLang router (`sgl-router` in front of `sglang.launch_server` workers) is derived from the same engine settings and was not measured.
- **KV offload.** The disaggregated prefill workers were measured with HiCache in host DRAM; the aggregated workers without KV offload. The other tiers (none, HiCache, or a Mooncake store attached through the external linker) are derived and were not measured.
- **Container image.** The commands show `<K31_IMAGE>`. The measured image was built with `docker/k31.Dockerfile` from SGLang commit `9ab3c28176` plus one unmerged change, and bundles FlashInfer 0.7.2, `sgl-deep-gemm` and a Dynamo 1.6.0 build with Kimi K3.1 Pro and session-affinity patches. `lmsysorg/sglang:dev-kimi-k31-pro` has not been checked against these commands.
- **Draft.** The commands load the [Kimi-K3.1-Dflash2-Preview](https://huggingface.co/RadixArk/Kimi-K3.1-Dflash2-Preview) draft by its Hugging Face id; the measured runs read a local copy of it.

On MI355X:

- **Shape.** One node up to 56 agents: TP8 on the monokernel for 1 and 4, TP8 · DCP8 with HiCache from 8. The 1P1D runs on two nodes.
- **Image.** `lmsysorg/sglang:dev-kimi-k31-pro-mi35x`; the recipes were measured on `sha256:5901db14`.

## 1. Model Introduction

**Kimi K3.1 Pro** is Moonshot AI's open-weight, natively multimodal agentic model and the first model in the K3.1 series, post-trained from Kimi-K3 with large-scale joint reinforcement learning. It is a **2.8T-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model with 104B activated**: 93 layers (1 dense) combining **69 KDA + 24 Gated Sparse-MLA (DSA)** attention layers, **896 experts with 16 selected per token plus 2 shared**, SiTU-GLU activations, a 160K vocabulary, the MoonViT-V2 vision encoder (401M) for image input, and a **1,048,576-token** context. Weights are **MXFP4 with MXFP8 activations** (quantization-aware training). SGLang serves it as `KimiK31ForConditionalGeneration` and forces FP8 KV with page size 64.

Kimi K3.1 Pro always thinks; set the depth with the top-level `reasoning_effort` field: `low`, `high` or `max` (default `max`). For multi-turn conversations and tool calls, pass the complete assistant message back in `messages` as-is, including `reasoning_content` and `tool_calls`, not just `content`.

**Recommended generation:** `temperature=1.0`, `top_p=0.95` (fixed by the model; informational — do not hardcode in sample code).

**License:** [Kimi K3.1 Pro License](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro/blob/main/LICENSE).

**Resources:** [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro) · [Tech Blog](https://www.kimi.ai/blog/kimi-k3-1-pro) · [API Quickstart](https://platform.kimi.ai/docs/guide/kimi-k3-1-pro-quickstart) · [DFLASH draft](https://huggingface.co/RadixArk/Kimi-K3.1-Dflash2-Preview).

## 2. Configuration Tips

### Recipe shapes

- **GB300**: every recipe runs on two trays (2×4 GPUs over MNNVL); the command adds the multi-node flags.
- **MI350X / MI355X**: one 8-GPU node per server; the 1P1D runs on two nodes (see [AMD MI350X / MI355X](#amd-tips)).
- **PD Mode**
  - `Unified` serves prefill and decode together.
  - `Prefill` / `Decode` split them (see [PD disaggregation](#pd-disaggregation)).
- **Prefill strategy** (B300, GB300)
  - `Low-Latency (TP8)` is flat TP8.
  - `High-Throughput (PP4 × PCP2)` is PP4 × PCP2 with MegaMoE EP2 (the cell carries MegaMoE; switch the MoE runner in the Advanced Features Playground): the most prefill tokens per second, and it serves the 1M window.
- **Unified strategy**
  - `Low-Latency (TP8)` (B300, GB300, MI350X, MI355X) is flat TP8.
  - `High-Concurrency (DCP8)` (B300, GB300) is TP8 · DCP8 · EP2.
  - `High-Throughput (DCP8)` (MI350X, MI355X) is TP8 · DCP8 over the a2a backend.
  - `Balanced` (other platforms) is the placeholder shape for each platform; the parentheses give its layout: TP8 on Vera Rubin, TP16 · DCP16 on B200 and GB200, TP16 · EP16 on H200, TP32 · EP32 on H100, TP32 · EP8 on TPU7x.
- **Decode strategy**
  - `Balanced (DCP8 · EP2)` is TP8 · DCP8 · EP2.
  - `High-Throughput (DCP8)` is the capacity-first decode: TP8 · DCP8, measured on B300 without speculation.
- **Attention Backend** (B300, GB300, B200, GB200; in the Advanced Features Playground)
  - `FlashMLA` is the default DSA kernel and keeps the reference FP8 KV format.
  - `TRT-LLM` and `Prims-TS` add `--dsa-prefill-backend` / `--dsa-decode-backend`. They store the KV as plain FP8 and also run the query in FP8, so the numerics differ slightly from FlashMLA.
  - With PD disaggregation, launch the prefill and decode roles with the same backend.
- **MoE Runner** (B300, GB300, B200, GB200; in the Advanced Features Playground)
  - `TRT-LLM` is the default: the FlashInfer trtllm-gen fused MXFP4 MoE, with no extra flag.
  - `MegaMoE` adds `--moe-a2a-backend megamoe`, which sets EP to the TP size. On the flat TP cells it also adds `--shared-experts-tp-size` and the `SGLANG_OPT_DEEPGEMM_MEGA_MOE_NUM_MAX_TOKENS_PER_RANK` cap (16384 for prefill and unified, 4096 for decode).
  - MegaMoE has only been measured on the High-Throughput (PP4 × PCP2) prefill; TRT-LLM on the other cells.
  - MegaMoE reserves a symmetric workspace that grows with EP size × the token cap; lower `--mem-fraction-static` if startup runs out of memory.
- **Spec Decode**
  - DFLASH with the [Kimi-K3.1-Dflash2-Preview](https://huggingface.co/RadixArk/Kimi-K3.1-Dflash2-Preview) draft.
  - Block 8; block 6 on the High-Throughput (DCP8) decode.
- **HiCache**
  - Off by default; L2 and L3 keep DCP on Kimi K3.1 Pro.
  - On a PD decode server HiCache also turns on the decode radix cache.
  - On MI350X / MI355X, `L1+L2 (host)` is the default except on Low-Latency and the PD decode (see [AMD MI350X / MI355X](#amd-tips)).

### Tuning notes

- **Memory.** The KDA state pool (FP32, ~52 MB per request per rank at TP8) sets the concurrency ceiling; the MLA + indexer KV (~18.9 KB per token) fills the rest. TP and PCP replicate the MLA KV; DCP is the only axis that shards it.
- **DFLASH.** Decode needs `--enable-linear-replayssm-spec` (the cells carry it) and an explicit `--max-running-requests`. On NVIDIA, the draft KV ring is on by default and adds ~15% target KV.
- **MegaMoE.** Non-prefill roles must set `--max-running-requests`; do not add `--enable-symm-mem` (it deadlocks).
- **PP prefill.** Keep `SGLANG_PP_LAYER_PARTITION=24,24,24,21` and `CUDA_MODULE_LOADING=EAGER` (both in the High-Throughput prefill cell).
- **HiCache.** Use `--hicache-io-backend direct --hicache-mem-layout page_first_direct` with `--mamba-max-states-per-path 1` (the HiCache overlay adds them).

<a id="amd-tips" />

### AMD MI350X / MI355X

- **ROCm 10** only. MI350X runs the MI355X commands.
- **By agent count.** 1-4: `Unified` `Low-Latency`, on the monokernel (no HiCache); at 1 agent run 1 request. 8-56: `Unified` `High-Throughput` with HiCache, at its 48-agent shape; at 56 agents run 56 requests, and below 48 use 16k chunks with 6, 8, 13 and 20 requests at 8, 16, 24 and 32 agents. Beyond that, the 1P1D (see [On AMD MI355X](#pd-amd)).
- **DFLASH.** Triton draft attention and block verification; block 8 at 1-4 agents, 6 at 8, and 5 from 16 agents and on both PD roles.
- **HiCache.** The pinned host pools share the node's GTT limit (1511 GiB on MI355X); larger ratios fail at startup.

### 3.3 PD Disaggregation

- **Transfer**: Mooncake (DCP rejects other backends on either role). Prefill listens on `30000`, decode on `30100`.
- **When using DFLASH, run it on both roles**: the prefill writes the draft KV ring that decode reads.
- **Restart the prefill role whenever the decode role restarts**: prefill keeps the old decode's Mooncake mapping.

```bash Command

  --no-tokenizer \
  --worker-urls http://<prefill-host>:30000 http://<decode-host>:30100 \
  --host 0.0.0.0 --port 8000 \
  --policy power_of_two \
  --stream-idle-timeout-secs 3600 \
  --stale-request-timeout-secs 3600 \
  --request-timeout-secs 540
```

- **Container**: add `--device=/dev/infiniband --cap-add=IPC_LOCK --ulimit memlock=-1 --network=host`. On Pollara (Ionic) NICs also mount the host's `libionic-rdmav34.so` and `/etc/libibverbs.d/ionic.driver`, as the panel's commands do; without them Mooncake moves KV over TCP.

### 3.4 TPU7x deployment

**Kimi K3.1 Pro** ([`moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro`](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro)) is Kimi-K3 with a DSA sparse indexer (top-2048) on each of its 24 MLA
layers. On TPU it runs on the same 2x2x4 slice as Kimi-K3 ([Kimi-K3 TPU deployment](/cookbook/autoregressive/Moonshotai/Kimi-K3#tpu)): 16 tpu7x chips on
four hosts (4 chips x 2 TensorCores each), one engine, tp32/ep8. The MoE weights
stay in the checkpoint's MXFP4 and the MLA KV cache is fp8 (`fp8_e4m3`).
Validated with `torch_tpu` 0.2.0 and `libtpu` 0.0.50.

<Note>
**Highlights** (93 layers, 16 tpu7x chips, one request unless stated):
- **~133 tok/s** decode at 8K context and **~112 tok/s at 128K**: decode speed holds across the context window.
- **~530 tok/s** with the decode megakernel + DFlash on GSM8K (experimental, section 3.4.4), and **~700 tok/s** at an acceptance length of 6.
- **5.1 s** TTFT for a 128K prompt with a 90% prefix-cache hit; **~11K tok/s** (input + output) at 128K with 2 concurrent sessions.
- Accuracy: **AIME25 30/30**, **MATH-500 98.4%**, **GPQA-diamond 93.4%**, **HumanEval 98.2%**, needle-in-a-haystack **100%** up to 128K.
</Note>

**Docker image:** `lmsysorg/sglang:dev-kimi-k31-pro-tpu` (SGLang with the TPU
plugin, `torch_tpu` and `libtpu` preinstalled). Pull it on all four hosts and run
the commands below inside it, with host networking and access to the TPU devices:

```bash
docker pull lmsysorg/sglang:dev-kimi-k31-pro-tpu
docker run -it --privileged --net=host --ipc=host \
    -v ~/.cache/huggingface:/root/.cache/huggingface \
    lmsysorg/sglang:dev-kimi-k31-pro-tpu bash
```

The commands load `moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro` from Hugging Face, so the first
start on each host downloads about 1.5 TB of weights into the mounted cache. To
avoid the wait, download them on every host beforehand (also the DFlash draft
if you use section 3.4.2):

```bash
huggingface-cli download moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro
huggingface-cli download RadixArk/Kimi-K3.1-Dflash2-Preview   # only for section 3.4.2
```

As for Kimi-K3, every host runs the same command, adding its own
`--node-rank <0-3> --dist-init-addr <node0-ip>:20000`. With the recipe launcher
(`python3 -m benchmark.tpu.launch_server`), the node rank and the rank-0
address come from `TPU_WORKER_ID` and `TPU_WORKER_HOSTNAMES` instead.

#### 3.4.1 Native server (128K context)

The Kimi-K3 TPU server plus the K3.1 sparse-MLA backend, fp8 KV, radix cache,
the per-op decode flags, four host-side decode launch switches
(`SGLANG_TPU_DECODE_SKIP_GUARD_EVAL`, `SGLANG_TPU_AOT_LEAN_RUNTIME_WRAPPER`,
`SGLANG_TPU_SPLIT_EXEC_DIRECT_CALL`, `SGLANG_TPU_PALLAS_VMEM_RIGHTSIZE`; validated
on K3.1 only, so the K3 commands leave them off) and the DSA indexer token split, sized for a 128K
context (131072 in + 8192 out) and up to 64 running requests. Prefill is
chunked at 4096 tokens, the one compiled EXTEND bucket, and a chunk carries up
to 8 requests. 256K (262144) context has also been tested.

The command is generated from `benchmark/tpu/recipe/configs/kimi_k3_1/server.py`
(change the recipe, not the page).

What the K3.1-specific settings do (each `SGLANG_TPU_*` switch is off by
default; the Kimi-K3 ones are described in [Kimi-K3 TPU deployment](/cookbook/autoregressive/Moonshotai/Kimi-K3#tpu)):

| Setting | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `--attention-backend pallas_tpu_mla_sparse` | Sparse MLA with the DSA indexer on the 24 indexed layers. |
| `--kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3` | fp8 MLA KV cache. |
| `--max-total-tokens 4000000` | An upper bound only: the pool is sized by `--mem-fraction-static`, about 532K tokens on 16 chips. MLA KV and DSA index keys are replicated on every rank. |
| `SGLANG_TPU_ENABLE_DSA_ATTENTION_TOKEN_SPLIT` | Each of the 32 ranks attends only its own slice of a prefill chunk, with all 96 heads: a Pallas all-to-all moves the queries and outputs, so each selected KV row is gathered by one rank instead of all 32. Cuts cold TTFT by 21-23% at 32K-128K. |
| `SGLANG_TPU_ENABLE_QBLOCK_SPARSE_MLA_PREFILL` | Query-blocked sparse MLA prefill for the DSA layers. |
| `SGLANG_TPU_VL_TEXT_ONLY_EXTEND` | Compiles only the token-id prefill graphs (text serving), which saves host memory at startup. |
| `SGLANG_TPU_TWOLEVEL_ALLREDUCE_SPLIT_BARRIER`, `SGLANG_TPU_K3_QKVG_PREFETCH`, `SGLANG_TPU_K3_LATENT_UP_ATTN_RES`, `SGLANG_TPU_K3_OPROJ_ROW` | Per-op decode kernels (all-reduce barrier split, KDA qkvg weight prefetch, attention residual fused into latent-up, row-split o_proj). |
| `SGLANG_TPU_DYNAMO_PARAMS_AS_GRAPH_ATTRS=2`, `SGLANG_TPU_ALLOC_DECODE_FAST_PATH` | Cut host overhead per decode step; at batch 1 decode on 93 layers is host bound. |
| `SGLANG_TPU_LOGITS_ONESHOT_GATHER` | One-shot Pallas all-gather of the decode logits. |
| `xla_tpu_offload_gather_to_sparsecore=true` | Runs the DSA top-k gathers on the SparseCore. |
| `xla_tpu_buffer_assignment_enable_fast_merge_fallback=false` | Needed with large KV pools on libtpu before 0.0.51. |

#### 3.4.2 Optional: DFlash speculative decoding (one request)

On top of section 3.4.1, with the Kimi-K3.1 DFlash draft
[`RadixArk/Kimi-K3.1-Dflash2-Preview`](https://huggingface.co/RadixArk/Kimi-K3.1-Dflash2-Preview)
(8 tokens per verify: 7 proposals plus the anchor), for single-stream latency:
**5.34 ms TPOT (~187 tok/s)** at 8K, with a mean accept length of 4.0. It serves
one request at a time (`--max-running-requests 1`); requests that ask for
logprobs are answered with a 400.

The draft model uses MLA; the server expands its latent and serves it as
multi-head attention, so the draft attention backend is `pallas_tpu_rpa_v3`.

For long-context (64K-128K) serving, the native server (3.4.1) is recommended.

```bash
export SGLANG_TPU_ENABLE_EXPERIMENTAL_SPEC_WORKER=1
export SGLANG_RAGGED_VERIFY_MODE=static
export SGLANG_SPEC_TP_SYNC=rng
export SGLANG_DFLASH_COLUMN_PARALLEL_FC=1

# Add to the section 3.4.1 command (replacing its batch settings):
    --max-running-requests 1 \
    --torch-compile-max-bs 1 \
    --tpu-compile-bs 1 \
    --speculative-algorithm DFLASH \
    --speculative-draft-model-path RadixArk/Kimi-K3.1-Dflash2-Preview \
    --speculative-num-draft-tokens 8 \
    --speculative-draft-attention-backend pallas_tpu_rpa_v3 \
    --speculative-draft-kv-cache-dtype bf16
```

`SGLANG_DFLASH_COLUMN_PARALLEL_FC` shards the draft's context projection over
TP (column-parallel, gathered output).

#### 3.4.3 Image input

The section 3.4.1 server is text only. For image input, generate the command from
`benchmark/tpu/recipe/configs/kimi_k3_1/server_vl.py`: the section 3.4.1 command with
`SGLANG_TPU_VL_TEXT_ONLY_EXTEND=0` (it also compiles the image prefill graphs),
`SGLANG_TPU_ENABLE_VISION_SPLASH_ATTENTION=1`, and at most 32 running requests
(`--max-running-requests 32 --torch-compile-max-bs 32 --tpu-compile-bs 1 2 4 8 16 32`).
The Kimi-K3 processor admits images of up to 65536 patches; the default vision
attention does not compile above about 16K patches, while splash attention
precompiles the vision encoder up to 66564 patches.

Measured on 16 chips with `reasoning_effort` max: OCRBench **0.874** (all 1000)
and MMMU-Pro (standard, 10 options) **0.860** on a 50-question subset. Startup
takes about 37 minutes, and free host memory drops to about 160 GB per host
while it compiles.

#### 3.4.4 Measured performance

Four tpu7x hosts (16 chips), all 93 layers, the native server (3.4.1) without
speculative decoding unless stated.

Measured in November 2026 on four tpu7x hosts (16 chips), all 93 layers.

Single-stream decode (8k in / 1k out; the megakernel rows use short prompts):

| Configuration | TPOT | Decode speed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Native (3.4.1) | 7.52 ms | **~133 tok/s** |
| Native + DFlash (3.4.2) | 5.34 ms | **~187 tok/s** |
| Decode megakernel + DFlash (experimental), GSM8K, mean accept length 4.4 | 1.88 ms | **~530 tok/s** |
| Decode megakernel + DFlash (experimental), accept length fixed at 6 | 1.42 ms | **~700 tok/s** |

Long context, one request:

| Context | Decode TPOT | Decode speed | TTFT, 90% prefix-cache hit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 64K | 8.6 ms | **~116 tok/s** | 2.3 s |
| 128K | 8.9 ms | **~112 tok/s** | 5.1 s |

With the DSA indexer token split, a cold 64K / 128K prompt prefills in 13.5 /
27.0 s (20-29% faster than without it).

Multi-turn / agentic serving (90% prefix-cache hit), within a TPOT p90 of 30 ms:

| Context | Concurrency | TTFT p90 | TPOT p90 | Throughput (input + output) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8K | 16 | 5.5 s | 28.5 ms | ~4.7K tok/s |
| 32K | 8 | 7.3 s | 27.6 ms | ~9.0K tok/s |
| 64K | 4 | 7.5 s | 24 ms | ~10.0K tok/s |
| 128K | 2 | 6.4 s | 21.6 ms | ~11.0K tok/s |

The fixed-acceptance row follows the usual speculative-decoding benchmark
convention (`SGLANG_SIMULATE_ACC_LEN=6`): it measures speed at that acceptance
length, not output quality. The decode megakernel runs all 93 decoder layers as
one Pallas call per step (see the Kimi-K3 TPU deployment); for K3.1 it also runs the DSA indexer
inside the kernel. It is experimental and serves one request at a time.
